The oatmeal test

Kate Compton's warning: 10,000 different bowls of oatmeal still look like oatmeal. Variety has to land on what you notice at 30 pixels, which is hair shape, the top's colour and the accessories. If any two people in this grid read as the same person, the generator needs work.

Palette

Colours that belong together

Every material is a ramp of five tones built in OKLCH, where equal lightness looks equally light. Shadows turn cooler and highlights warmer instead of just darker and lighter, so shading glows rather than going muddy.

How it holds together

Rules the generator follows

Hair stands out from skin

Hair and skin differ by at least 0.12 in lightness, or the head reads as one blob at game size.

Outfits split in two

Top and bottom differ by 0.15 in lightness, and stripes, logos and collars by 0.2 from the top, so details stay visible.

One light, top left

Every shape is its shadow tone with the base tone nudged up and left. People, desks and props are lit the same way.

Hats respect silhouettes

An afro, a mohawk or a bun never gets a hat. The hairstyle is the most recognisable shape at 30 pixels.

Desks follow their owner

Each desk has five anchor slots. The owner's desk vibe picks the props and their outfit colours the mug, pot and headphones. Free desks stay bare and dark.

Headsets mean something

Headphones move from the desk to the head when someone joins a call, so the picture shows who can hear you.
